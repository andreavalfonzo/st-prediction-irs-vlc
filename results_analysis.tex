\section{Results and analysis}
\label{sec:resultados}

Without retraining, we evaluated 16 held-out synthetic episodes per
scenario, with a 50~ms control delay. Over the common 3.00--5.95~s
window, CNN--GRU achieved 9.57~bits/s/Hz during normal motion,
compared with 9.64 for constant-velocity extrapolation.
Sudden stopping, stationary positioning and erratic motion yielded
9.69, 9.33 and 9.60~bits/s/Hz, respectively, without outperforming
reactive control.

The simulated fall, implemented as receiver descent and tilt,
reduced the CNN--GRU rate to 5.83~bits/s/Hz, with 43.96\%
of intervals below $R_{\min}=5$~bits/s/Hz; reactive control achieved
6.02~bits/s/Hz. The separate cylindrical blocker retained its
original trajectory across scenarios. Even instantaneous geometric
alignment retained 43.54\% outage, indicating that physical channel
limitations dominate this degradation. Constant-velocity control
reached only 1.60~bits/s/Hz in this case, but its fixed-height
assumption confounds the comparison. These results assess
communication robustness under synthetic perturbations; they do
not validate fall or anomaly detection.
